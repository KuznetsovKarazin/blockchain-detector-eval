## Suggested synthesis for the manuscript

Code duplication and data leakage are established threats to empirical model evaluation. DéjàVu documented duplication across source repositories, and Allamanis studied its consequences for models of code. Kaufman et al. and Kapoor and Narayanan provide broader accounts of leakage relative to prediction goals and research practice. Our contribution should therefore not be framed as discovering duplication or proposing train/test separation.

Vulnerability-detection research has also examined data quality and construct validity. Chakraborty et al. and Croft et al. connect benchmark artifacts and annotation problems to unreliable evaluation. PrimeVul combines labeling, deduplication and temporal evaluation. Risse and colleagues show why spurious features, patch insensitivity and missing context can survive apparently successful classification. These studies motivate our interpretation of proxy-label AP as benchmark agreement rather than confirmed vulnerability recognition.

For Solidity, Kondo et al. establish the importance of cloning and library reuse. SmartBugs, SolidiFI and DAppSCAN provide different evaluation resources; their annotation procedures are not interchangeable. More recently, Salzano et al. offer manually annotated line-level cases, while Khalid et al. compare tools across canonical and audit-derived benchmarks. The narrower contribution supported by our current artifacts is a controlled examination of how the source population used to build transitive dependencies changes valid training eligibility on a fixed original population. The A–D experiment distinguishes exhaustive edge discovery on the historical population from adding uncapped reference files and then external-candidate files. It is a corpus-specific graph result, not proof of semantic identity, a new leakage definition, or a completed external tool evaluation.
